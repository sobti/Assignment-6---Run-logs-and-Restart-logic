The belay parka is a pretty specialized pieced of a mountain climber's kit: It's the super-warm coat you throw on when you're belaying your climbing partner.
Climbing is a start-and-stop sport, one where you generate lots of heat when you're working to conquer a pitch. But then you lose that heat when you're spotting your fellow climber. The belay parka is an over-layer for when you're standing around, and it needs to keep you warm in very, very cold conditions.
The best material for insulation is still down, but there's one major problem – it's useless once it gets wet. But Patagonia is hoping to overcome that obstacle with its new Encapsil Down, which coats the bird fluff with a plasma treatment that changes the structure of the feathers.
This is the first parka to ever offer 1,000-fill-power down.There are two advantages to the treatment. The first is that the down becomes water resistant, although it's not completely waterproof. The second advantage is that the down actually becomes fluffier, which means that this is the first parka to ever offer 1,000-fill-power down.
That super-fluffy down keeps the parka's weight down to just 1.13 pounds, but still offers nearly overwhelming insulation. For those of us in the temperate Bay Area, it is barely wearable for more than a few minutes. But for mountaineers, or civilians in bitterly cold climates, it would be a home run. The cut and construction of the parka is exquisite, with a differential cut between the inner and outer shells, much like a sleeping bag, so the insulation is closer to your body with no cold or hot spots.
The Encapsil Down Belay Parka doesn't come cheap – retail price is a chill-inducing $700, and it's a limited-edition run of 1,000 jackets. But if you're looking for the ultimate cold-weather coat, this just might be it.
Available March 1 online and in select Patagonia stores.
