 New Delhi: After 29 days of inactivity, petrol and diesel prices were hiked on Wednesday. The retail price of petrol is on course to breach its all-time high level after it rose by 26 paise per litre today. It is now available for Rs 83. 97 per litre in Delhi while diesel went up by 25 paise per litre was retailed at Rs 74. 12 in the national capital.
 The resumption in price hike came as international oil prices reached an 11-month high owing to potential production cuts by Saudi Arabia. Before Wednesday’s move, fuel prices in India last increased on December 7.
 Petrol price in Delhi peaked at Rs 84 per litre on October 4, 2018, while diesel reached its all-time high level of Rs 81. 94 per litre on July 30, 2020.
 After Wednesday's rise, petrol price in Mumbai was recorded Rs 90. 60 per litre, Rs 85. 44 in Kolkata, Rs 86. 55 in Chennai and Rs 95. 8 in Bhopal.
 The Covid-19 pandemic took its toll on the global energy demand including India where the production and consumption of petroleum and petroleum products were impacted. However, the development of vaccines and the subsequent beginning of the inoculation programmes in many countries supported crude oil prices globally.
 On Wednesday, crude oil prices in the international markets rose to their highest levels since February 2020 after Saudi Arabia in a meeting with Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) members agreed to cut output more than expected.
 Brent crude rose 0. 9% to $54. 09 a barrel, its highest level in the 11 months. It was at $53. 82 a barrel at 0757 GMT after adding 4. 9% on Tuesday, according to Reuters. US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) futures were up 0. 6% at $50. 24 a barrel, highest since February 26, before slipping to $49. 96.
 World’s largest oil exporter Saudi Arabia in the OPEC+ meeting agreed to cut production upto 1 million barrels per day (bpd) in February and March.
 India’s state-run oil marketing companies such as Indian Oil Corporation, Bharat Petroleum Corporation Ltd and Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Ltd have been revising rates of petrol and diesel daily since 2017 based on benchmark international oil price and foreign exchange rate.
